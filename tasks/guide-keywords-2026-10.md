## Summary of Method Notes / Failures

- 6 exact seed phrases returned "Nothing found" in Semrush and were substituted with the nearest suggested variant (noted inline above): "who would win anime" → no usable substitute found (tried "anime vs", which skewed to low-volume manga-vs-anime-format queries, not character battles); "saitama vs" → no good substitute (One Punch Man crossover demand appears to route through "naruto vs" instead, which was already a seed); "naruto strongest" → used "naruto strongest character"; "hunter x hunter strongest" → used "strongest hunter x hunter character"; "strongest manhwa characters" → no manhwa-specific equivalent exists yet in Semrush's US index (only "strongest manga character" at low volume); "manhwa power system" → used "system manhwa".
- "anime ending explained" and generic "anime explained" returned very low, mostly non-commercial/meme-intent volume — this angle needs per-title pages ("[Anime] Ending Explained") rather than a generic hub; not enough seed-level data to recommend specific titles without further research.
- "best isekai anime" is a high-volume but very high-KD (60-81%) cluster — not recommended as a direct target; softer long-tail angles (reincarnation, fantasy romance) were substituted into the Top 40 list instead.
- Several seeds ("who would win anime", "saitama vs") simply have no measurable Semrush search volume for the exact or closely-related phrasing in the US database — this is a genuine data gap, not a tool failure.
