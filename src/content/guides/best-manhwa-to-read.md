Manhwa, Korean comics, have gone from a niche corner of the internet to some of the most read comics in the world in a few short years. That growth brings a problem: there are thousands of series, and the ones at the top of an app's chart are not always the best. This list picks fifteen manhwa worth your time, explains why each one stands out, and says who it suits, so you can skip straight to the one that fits you.

Every title here has an official English release, and all of them can be read legally on apps or in print. The title pages on this site link to each licensed platform.

## How we picked these manhwa

Three things decided the list. The first is quality over a long run: a series had to stay good past its opening arc, because most manhwa run for hundreds of chapters. The second is reach: each one is a title fans keep recommending years after it started, not a one-season trend. The third is range. Action fantasy dominates the charts, but it is not the only thing Korean comics do well, so the list also covers comedy, horror, school drama and romance fantasy.

The order is a reading recommendation, not a quality score. Titles near the top are the easiest to love on a first read, and titles further down are often just as good but aimed at a narrower taste.

## Which one should you start with?

**If you have never read a manhwa,** start with Solo Leveling. It shows off everything the format does well in one finished story.

**If you want action with a brain,** go to Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint or Tower of God. Both reward close reading and both have twists that recast what came before.

**If you want to laugh,** pick Eleceed or The Greatest Estate Developer. Return of the Blossoming Blade is the same kind of fun with sword fights.

**If you want romance,** True Beauty is the light one, The Remarried Empress the clever one, and Under the Oak Tree the slow and grown-up one.

**If you want something finished to binge,** Solo Leveling, Sweet Home and Noblesse can each be read in a few long sittings.

## Reading tips for new readers

Most manhwa are made to be read on a phone, one long vertical strip per chapter, in full colour. That is why the apps feel natural and a laptop screen can feel awkward. Many series also release a chapter a week and take seasonal breaks between arcs, so a long ongoing series is best started when you know there is a backlog.

Free reading on official apps usually works on a wait-or-pay model: older chapters unlock for free over time, and newer ones cost coins. Reading this way pays the artists and writers, and it keeps the series you love going.
