Jujutsu Kaisen sorts every sorcerer and every curse into the same set of grades, and the grades tell you who gets sent where. A first-year should not walk into a Grade 1 curse's lair; when one does, people die. This guide explains the JJK grade system from Grade 4 to Special Grade, who holds each rank, how promotion works, and how curse grades line up with sorcerer grades.

The short version: there are five main grades, Grade 4 at the bottom and Special Grade at the top, with semi-grades in between. Only four sorcerers in the story ever hold Special Grade.

> Spoiler note: this guide mentions events up to the Shibuya Incident and a few later rank changes, but avoids the details of the manga's final arcs.

## How the grade system works

Jujutsu sorcerers are graded by the jujutsu higher-ups, the old council that runs the jujutsu world. A grade does two jobs. It tells the school which missions a sorcerer can take, and it tells sorcerers how much danger to expect from a curse of a given grade. A Grade 2 sorcerer is meant to handle Grade 2 curses, a Grade 1 sorcerer Grade 1 curses, and so on.

The full sorcerer ladder, from lowest to highest:

| Grade | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Grade 4 | Beginners and sorcerers held back for any reason |
| Grade 3 | Most new students start here |
| Semi-Grade 2 / Grade 2 | Capable professionals |
| Semi-Grade 1 / Grade 1 | Elite sorcerers trusted with the most dangerous missions |
| Special Grade | Sorcerers beyond the normal scale; only four exist |

## Curse grades

Curses use the same ladder, and the manga gives a memorable comparison for what each grade takes to destroy with ordinary weapons. A Grade 4 curse could be beaten with a wooden bat. A Grade 3 curse would need about a pistol. A Grade 2 curse would call for a shotgun. A Grade 1 curse might survive a tank. A Special Grade curse could shrug off carpet bombing.

Most of the series' big villains are Special Grade curses, including the disaster curses Mahito, Jogo and Hanami. Sukuna stands above them all.

## How promotion works

Promotion is political. To move up, a sorcerer needs recommendations from established sorcerers of Grade 1 or higher, and then must prove themselves on assigned missions before the higher-ups sign off. That is how students can rise quickly when their teachers vouch for them, and how a powerful clan can hold someone down. Maki is the clearest case: the Zenin clan worked to keep her at Grade 4 no matter what she achieved.

Grades can also be lowered or suspended. Yuta was placed at Grade 4 when he arrived at Jujutsu High under a suspended death sentence, then restored to Special Grade once he could control Rika.

## Grade versus real strength

A grade is a record of what someone has proven, filtered through the politics of the jujutsu world. It is a good guide but not a ranking of strength. Yuji and Maki both outgrow their standing long before the system catches up, and Gojo sits so far above the rest that even Special Grade barely describes him.
