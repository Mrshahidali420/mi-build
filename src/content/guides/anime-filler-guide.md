Filler is the price of a long-running anime. When a weekly anime catches up with the weekly manga it adapts, the studio has two choices: stop, or make up new episodes until the manga is far enough ahead again. For years most chose to keep going, and the result is shows where hundreds of episodes are not part of the original story at all.

This guide explains what filler is, how it differs from canon, and how much of it each big long-running anime has, with the arcs worth skipping and the few worth keeping. Where a series has its own detailed list on this site, the entry links to it.

## What counts as filler

**Canon** episodes adapt the source manga, novel or game. **Filler** episodes are written for the anime alone and do not happen in the source. **Mixed canon** episodes adapt the manga but add a lot of new material to stretch it out. **Anime canon** is anime-original material that the anime's own story treats as real, such as a new character who keeps appearing. **Recap** episodes replay earlier events and can always be skipped.

Filler does not mean bad. Some filler arcs are fan favourites, because they give side characters more time or simply tell a fun story. What filler never does is move the main plot, so you can always skip it without getting lost.

## Why the big shows have so much

Naruto, Bleach, One Piece and Dragon Ball Z all aired every single week for years, with no seasonal breaks. A weekly manga chapter is roughly a third to half of an anime episode's story, so the anime catches up quickly. Filler was the way to keep the slot on air. Modern anime avoid the problem by airing in seasons with breaks between them, which is why most shows made in the last decade have no filler at all.

## How to use a filler list

Look up the series, then decide how strict to be. A strict watch skips every filler and mixed-canon episode that adds nothing. A relaxed watch keeps filler that people often recommend, such as One Piece's G-8 or the Shippuden episodes on Itachi's past, and skips the rest. Recaps can always go.

If you are watching to keep up with friends or the manga, the strict route saves the most time. Naruto and Shippuden together drop from 720 episodes to well under 450, which is weeks of watching saved. The [Naruto filler list](/guides/naruto-filler-list) gives every episode of both series.
