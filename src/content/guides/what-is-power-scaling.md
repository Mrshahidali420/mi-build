Power scaling is the hobby of working out how strong fictional characters are compared to each other. It is behind every "who is the strongest" list, every "who would win" thread and a lot of the arguments in anime comment sections. Done well, it is a careful reading of the story. Done badly, it is two people shouting numbers.

This guide explains what power scaling is, the terms you will see used, how a good scaling argument is built, and the mistakes that make one fall apart. Each idea comes with a famous anime character who shows it.

## What power scaling means

At its simplest, power scaling compares characters by what their stories show them doing. If Character A beats Character B, and B is shown destroying a mountain, then A is at least mountain level. String enough of those steps together and you get a ladder that places a whole cast.

Fans scale inside a single series, such as ranking the Hashira in Demon Slayer, and across series, such as Goku against Luffy. The first is far more reliable, because one author's rules hold throughout. The second is mostly for fun.

## The key terms

**Feats** are what a character is shown doing on the page or screen: cutting a building in half, outrunning lightning, surviving an explosion. They are the strongest evidence.

**Statements** are things the story says plainly, through narration or a trustworthy character, such as "Kaido is the strongest creature in the world".

**Hype** is how other characters talk about someone. It counts for less, because characters can be wrong or exaggerate.

**AP, or attack potency,** is how much destruction a character can cause. **Durability** is how much they can take. **Speed** is how fast they move and react. **Stamina** is how long they can keep fighting.

**Hax** is any power that ignores those stats: time stops, instant death, sealing, or barriers that nothing can pass.

**Tiers** are the bands scalers sort characters into, usually named after what they can destroy: wall, building, city, mountain, country, planet and beyond.

**Outliers** are feats far above or below a character's normal level. Good scalers set them aside.

**Composite** means merging every version of a character, from manga, anime, films and games, into one. Most careful rankings avoid it.

## How to build a good scaling argument

**Pick a version.** Name the point in the story you are judging. Every serious ranking on this site says which arcs it covers.

**Lead with feats.** Start from what the character did, then add statements, and use hype last.

**Use the source.** When the manga and anime disagree, most scalers follow the original. Filler, games and fan art do not count.

**Respect the author's rules.** If a series says a power cannot be blocked, that matters more than any number.

## Common mistakes

The most common mistake is **calculating everything**. Turning an explosion on the page into a number of tons of TNT is fun, but artists draw for drama, not physics, and one panel can give wildly different answers.

The next is **ignoring context**. A character who loses while poisoned, holding back or protecting someone is not weaker than the winner in every way.

The last is **mixing stories**. Comparing characters across series means comparing authors who never meant their worlds to meet. Enjoy those matchups, but hold the answers loosely.
