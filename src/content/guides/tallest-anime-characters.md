Height is one of the first things fans look up about a character, and one of the easiest to get wrong. Fan wikis and height charts often guess, round or mix up a character's size in different forms. This list only uses official heights, from the databooks, guidebooks, fanbooks and profiles published with each series, and says where each number comes from.

It ranks the tallest anime characters of human size, people who live in ordinary-sized worlds. Giants, kaiju, titans and characters who change size at will are judged at their normal height, or left out when no normal height exists.

## How this list works

Each entry gives the official height in centimetres, as it is published in Japan, with feet and inches next to it. The source type is listed for every one. When a character's height changes over the story, such as after a time skip, the list uses the most recent official figure unless noted.

Characters whose creators have never given a height, or who only have a vague one, are left off. Gojo is the best-known example: the official fanbook only says he is over 190 cm.

## Why One Piece dominates

The top eight places all belong to One Piece, and that is no accident. Oda designs his world with an enormous range of body types and has published heights for hundreds of characters through his question corners and the Vivre Card databooks. Most other series keep their cast within normal human sizes, so their tallest characters land around two metres.

If the list included giants, One Piece would take every spot anyway. The giants of Elbaph are many times taller than Big Mom.

## Who is left out, and why

**Giants and titans.** One Piece's giants, Attack on Titan's Titans and Dragon Ball's Great Apes are many times taller than anyone on this list. They are left off because the list compares people of roughly human scale, and a giant at fifteen metres would make every other number meaningless. For the Titans' official heights, see the guide to the nine Titans, where the Colossal Titan stands at 60 metres.

**Shapeshifters.** Piccolo can grow to giant size, Moria swells after absorbing shadows, and Kaido becomes a dragon. Each one is ranked at the normal height their official source gives.

**Characters without a number.** Many tall characters, such as Gojo, Sukuna's true form or the Demon Slayer Upper Moons in their monstrous states, have no exact official height. Rather than guess, the list leaves them out.

**Fan charts.** Height charts online often mix official numbers with estimates from panels. Artists draw for drama, not measurement, so a character can look a different height from scene to scene. Only published numbers count here.

## How to read the numbers

Japanese sources give heights in centimetres, and the feet and inches next to each entry are rounded conversions. Some series publish heights at more than one point in the story. Haikyu!! lists heights for each school year, which is why Lev's number is marked as his first-year figure, and One Piece updates several characters' heights after the time skip. Where two official numbers exist, this list uses the latest unless the entry says otherwise.

## Heights of popular main characters

For comparison, most shonen leads are of ordinary height. Monkey D. Luffy is 174 cm after the time skip, Goku 175 cm, and Levi Ackerman a famously short 160 cm. That gap is exactly why the characters above feel so imposing on screen.
