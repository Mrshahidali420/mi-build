Opening your first manga can be confusing. The book seems to start at the back, the panels jump around, and it is not obvious which speech bubble comes first. The good news is that it takes about ten pages to get used to. This guide explains how to read manga step by step: which way the pages go, how to follow panels and speech bubbles, what the sound effects are, how manhwa and webtoons differ, and where to read legally.

The short answer: **read manga right to left.** Start at the top right of each page and work your way to the bottom left.

## Step 1: Open the book from the "back"

Japanese books are bound on the right, so the first page of a manga is where an English book would end. If the first thing you see is a page saying you are reading the wrong way, flip the book over and start from the other cover. Digital readers handle this for you, but you will still swipe from left to right to go forward.

## Step 2: Read the pages right to left

On a two-page spread, read the right-hand page first, then the left. On each page, start at the top right corner.

## Step 3: Follow the panels in a backwards Z

Panels go in rows. Read the top row from right to left, then drop down to the next row and read it right to left, and so on to the bottom. If you trace your path with a finger, it looks like a "Z" drawn backwards. When a tall panel stretches down the side of the page, finish the panels next to it from top to bottom before moving on.

Artists design pages to guide your eye, so if a layout looks unusual, follow the flow of the action. A punch thrown from right to left usually leads you to the next panel.

## Step 4: Read speech bubbles top right first

Inside a panel, the same rule applies. Read the bubble nearest the top right first, then work down and to the left. A bubble that sits higher always comes before one lower down. Thought bubbles and narration boxes follow the same order.

## Step 5: Know the sound effects

Manga are full of sound effects drawn right into the art, such as "DON" for a dramatic moment or "GOGOGO" for a building sense of menace. Most English editions translate them in small text beside the original lettering. You can read them or skip them; they set the mood rather than carry the story.

## Chapters, volumes and simulpubs

Manga come out in **chapters**, usually weekly or monthly in a magazine such as Weekly Shonen Jump or in a publisher's app. Every few months, around eight to twelve chapters are collected into a **volume**, or tankobon, the paperback books you see in shops. When a series is released in English the same day as in Japan, that is called a **simulpub**, and it is how most popular series are read today.

## Manga, manhwa and manhua

**Manga** are Japanese comics, read right to left and usually in black and white. **Manhwa** are Korean comics. In print they read left to right, but most are now **webtoons**: full-colour comics made for phones that you read by scrolling down. **Manhua** are Chinese comics, which today are also mostly published as vertical webtoons. If you start with a webtoon, there is no direction to learn at all; you simply scroll.

## Where to read manga legally

Reading on official platforms pays the artists and keeps series going. **Manga Plus** by Shueisha offers the latest chapters of One Piece, Jujutsu Kaisen and many others free. The **Shonen Jump** app has a large back catalogue for a small subscription. **K Manga** carries Kodansha's series, and **Webtoon** and **Tapas** host thousands of manhwa and webtoons, many free to read. Public libraries lend printed volumes too.

Every series page on manhwaindex lists the official places to read it in your country, with links checked every six hours, so you never need an unofficial site.

## Tips for your first week

Read slowly for the first chapter, then let your eye take over. Do not worry about every sound effect. If a series feels hard to follow, try a quieter one first and come back to it. And if you already watch anime, starting with the manga of a show you know is an easy way in: you already know the characters, so you can focus on learning the page.
